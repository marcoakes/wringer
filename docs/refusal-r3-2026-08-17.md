# The human interlock, filmed — R3 / OQ-1, 2026-08-17

*Unedited console from `~/Claude/…/scratchpad/film_r3.sh`, driven against the
commit that flipped `accept.EMIT_V3` to True. No network: `origin` is a bare
repository on disk, and `--send` is never passed, so nothing was published.*

**What this films is a policy REVERSAL.** Until this commit a `human: true`
criterion never refused a delivery. It was the author's honest out: mark a
requirement as something only a person can judge, and it stopped mattering.
Three parts, in order.

## Part 1 — the delivery is REFUSED because nobody has answered

The gates pass. `csv-downloads` is `evidenced` — it was born red, a change
made it green, and the record shows the gate failing. And the delivery is
still refused, because `copy-reads-well` is a required human criterion and
**nobody has said anything about it.**

Three things to notice in that refusal block:

- The reason **names the file to edit**. Before this cycle a human row's reason
  was *"answered by people, not gates"*, which under a refusal heading is a
  non-sequitur — it tells you what kind of thing it is, not what to do.
- **The gate-remedy trailer is ABSENT.** Every other acceptance refusal ends
  with *"a criterion is evidenced when its gate passed AND the record shows
  that gate can fail"*. That is false guidance here: there is no gate, and no
  amount of evidence clears it. It now prints only when a refusing row is
  bound. Printing a remedy that cannot clear the refusal it prints under is a
  defect `deliver.py` already records fixing once.
- **The refusal wrote a record** — `.wringer/refusals/<id>/refusal.json`,
  R1's machinery, with `reason: acceptance_unevidenced` and the message
  verbatim.

The record itself is `wringer.acceptance.v3`, and the human row carries
`cause: human-unanswered`.

## Part 2 — a person answers, by hand, and the delivery proceeds

`wringer.judgements.yaml` is written **by a person**. There is no flag, no
`--judge`, no `--accept-human` and no environment variable that writes it, and
`test_no_flag_no_env_var_and_no_command_can_write_a_judgement` checks
structurally that nothing in `src/` does. A `human: true` criterion exists
precisely because a model asked anyway would be guessing.

`wring verify` is re-run before `wring deliver`, and that is not ceremony:
acceptance is computed at VERIFY time and read from the bundle at deliver
time, so editing the file and re-running `deliver` alone would film nothing.

The row now reads `refuses=false` — and its **state is still `human`**. It did
not become `evidenced`, and that is not a technicality: `evidenced` means a
bound check passed now and the record shows the same check recorded failing. A
person saying yes has no receipt. Rendering it under the same word would put a
human judgement inside the sentence "every green was red first".

The record gains one line in `limits[]`, in the engine's own voice, and it says
the weak part out loud: **the judgement is not re-checked by anything, and
later work can invalidate it without this record changing.** `limits[]` renders
verbatim on the board, so that reaches a PM with no translation to maintain.

Note also what got delivered: **2 files**. The judgements file travels into the
delivery commit. That is R3's decision, made rather than defaulted — a delivery
citing a human answer should carry the answer it cites, or a reviewer cannot
check the citation.

## Part 3 — the requirement is reworded, and it refuses again

The spec's criterion title changes. **The judgements file is untouched — it
still says `verdict: met`.** The delivery refuses anyway, under
`human-judgement-stale`: somebody answered a different question.

`stale` is COMPUTED at acceptance time from the digest, never read from the
file. A stale flag a person can write is a stale flag a person can forget.

## What this does NOT show

- **The judgement is pinned to the WORDING and to nothing else** — not to a
  tree, a commit, a bundle or a build. A person judged a running product at a
  moment; later work can break what they approved and **nothing here detects
  that.** It is in the record's own `limits[]` above. Pinning to the tree would
  invalidate every judgement on every commit, which is a constant-no wearing a
  rigour costume; the honest mechanism for "the thing I approved has regressed"
  is the ratchet.
- **`by` is recorded and never verified.** It is not an identity system.
- Nothing here was published. `--send` was never passed.
