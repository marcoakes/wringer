# Cold-reading the board — the sheet

A cold read is one person, one page, no explanation. It is the only check on
whether this surface does the thing it claims, and it cannot be replaced by
anyone who already knows what the words mean.

**The proxy run of 2026-08-19 used models prompted as people** — see
`2026-08-19-six-readers.md`. That is a lower bound on confusion, not a
measurement of it. **A human run is still owed.**

## Who counts as a stranger

Somebody who has never seen this tool, is not being paid by this project, and
has no reason to be kind about it. A product manager, a founder, a designer,
a developer on their first week. **Not** anyone who has read the README.

## How to run it

1. Send them **one file**: a rendered `board.html`. Nothing else — no link to
   the repository, no sentence of setup, no "this is a tool that…".
2. Say exactly this and no more: *"Someone handed you this page. What does it
   tell you?"*
3. **Do not answer questions during the read.** Every question they ask is the
   finding. Write it down verbatim and say you will come back to it.
4. Then ask, in this order:
   - In your own words, what is this page telling you?
   - Is the work finished? How sure are you?
   - If this were your project, what would you do next?
   - Point at anything you could not understand.
   - Is there anything here you think is not true?

## What to write down

Their words, not your summary of them. A cold read is destroyed by the
listener translating; the whole point is that the page failed to.

Record the **question they asked first**. On the proxy run every reader
converged on the same one — *are these eight checks the same thing as these
eight requirements or not?* — and that question is worth more than the rest of
the transcript.

## What counts as a pass

Not "they liked it". A pass is:

- they can say whether the work is finished, **and they are right**;
- they can say what would happen next without being told;
- they do not ask what a word means.

The proxy run failed all three. **Nobody said finished**, three said "partly",
three said "not finished", and every reader produced a list of words they had
to guess at.

## What this sheet is not

It is not a usability study and does not need to be one. One stranger, one
page, five questions. If it takes longer than twenty minutes, the page has
already answered the question.
